# Subject coverage — focused areas and knowledge type per subject

Two jobs for this file: (1) name the areas within each subject that
actually get tested, so a batch on "Polity" or "Economy" doesn't wander into
thin, rarely-tested corners while ignoring the core; (2) flag which subjects
lean on pure factual recall versus which regularly demand application,
situational, or analytical thinking — because that changes which format
tier (see `format-evolution.md`) fits naturally.

**This list is additive, not a ceiling.** As more papers get analysed, add
newly-confirmed areas and refine the fact-vs-analytical read — but never
quietly drop a focused area already confirmed here just because a recent
batch happened not to touch it. The areas below are what's been confirmed
from real papers (2023-2026) plus established syllabus weight; treat gaps as
"not yet confirmed," not "not tested."

## Predominantly factual — established, book-knowledge recall

These subjects mostly test whether a fact is known and precisely correct,
not whether it can be applied. Statement-based and pairs/rows formats
(Tier 1–2) are the natural home; Tier 3 formats should be used sparingly and
only when the content genuinely supports synthesis (e.g. identify-from-clues
built on a well-documented monument or dynasty).

- **Ancient History** — sources & archaeology (excavations, texts),
  major dynasties and their administration, religious movements
  (Buddhism/Jainism), art & architecture of the period.
- **Medieval History** — Delhi Sultanate and Mughal administration,
  regional kingdoms, Bhakti/Sufi movements, architecture, revenue systems.
- **Modern Indian History** — colonial policy and its economic/social
  effects, reform movements, freedom movement organisations and leaders,
  key legislative Acts, post-independence integration.
- **Art & Culture** — dance/music forms and their regions/gharanas,
  monuments and the dynasties/periods that built them, literature and
  authors, crafts, awards and honours.

## Mixed — factual core, but real application/analytical share

These subjects test foundational facts *and* regularly require applying a
rule, reasoning about cause-and-effect, or interpreting a scenario — which
is exactly where Statement-I/II, "how many," and (for the richer topics)
Tier 3 formats fit best.

- **Polity** — constitutional bodies and their functions/appointing
  authority (heavy pairs/rows territory), Fundamental Rights/Duties/DPSP,
  amendments, Parliament and legislative procedure, judiciary and judicial
  review, federalism and Centre-State relations, Panchayati Raj and local
  government, elections and the Election Commission. Application shows up
  as "does this provision actually apply in this situation," not just "what
  does the provision say."
- **Economy** — monetary policy and banking (RBI tools, inflation),
  fiscal policy and the Union Budget, growth/development indicators (GDP,
  employment, poverty measures), external sector (trade, BoP, exchange
  rate), agriculture economy, government schemes. Frequently asks you to
  reason about the *effect* of a policy or indicator change, not just
  define it — this is where Statement-I/II shows up most.
- **Geography** — physical geography (landforms, drainage, climate),
  economic/resource geography, map-based location questions, and
  cause-effect questions linking climate/terrain to outcomes (settlement
  patterns, agriculture, disaster risk). Heavy on pairs/rows (place ↔
  feature) but the physical-process questions demand real understanding,
  not just place-names.
- **Environment** — species and their conservation status/habitat,
  protected area network, international conventions and India's
  commitments, ecological cause-effect (a change in one factor's downstream
  effect), current-affairs-driven (recent reports, COP outcomes). Strong
  candidate for "how many of the above are true" given how many
  species/fact clusters exist.
- **Science & Tech** — space missions and agencies, defence
  technology, biotechnology and health, emerging tech (AI, semiconductors),
  and, increasingly, mechanism-level questions (how something works, why an
  effect follows a cause) — this subject takes to
  `relationship-among-statements` and Statement-I/II particularly well
  because scientific claims naturally chain into cause-effect relationships.
- **Agriculture** — cropping patterns and seasons, government schemes
  and MSP mechanics, agri-technology, allied sectors (dairy, fisheries).
  Mostly factual with situational application (which scheme/practice suits
  which situation).
- **International Relations / Events** — international organisations
  and India's memberships, summits and agreements, bilateral/regional
  dynamics, reports and rankings from global bodies. Prime candidate for the
  cross-topic/common-thread format — international bodies share structural
  attributes (headquarters, reporting lines, membership rules) across
  otherwise unrelated topics, which is exactly the shape that format needs.
- **Social Issues** — welfare schemes, social indicators and indices,
  demographic trends, health/education policy. Mostly factual with some
  applied (interpreting what an indicator implies).

## Using this alongside format selection

When a request names a subject without a specific topic, pick content from
the focused areas listed above rather than an obscure corner of the
syllabus, and let the fact-vs-analytical read guide which format tier to
lean on by default — a "make some Ancient History questions" request should
skew Tier 1–2 and pairs-heavy; a "make some Economy questions" request has
real room for Statement-I/II and, where the content supports it, Tier 3.
