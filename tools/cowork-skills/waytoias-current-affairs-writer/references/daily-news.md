# Daily current affairs — `daily_current_affairs`

One article per distinct story — if the user gives you several unrelated
stories in one request, write and file them as separate articles, not one
piece with several stories glued together.

## Structure

Use these sections, in this order. Omit a section only when the material
genuinely does not support it — don't pad one out to keep the shape.

1. **Why in News** — the trigger event and its date, in two or three sentences.
2. **Background** — the minimum context a reader needs to follow the story.
   Don't over-explain things most aspirants already know.
3. **Key Facts** — bullet points. Exact figures, dates, Article/Section
   numbers, official scheme names. This is the section students will actually
   scan for revision, so density matters more than prose style here.
4. **Significance** — why it matters: governance, economy, society,
   international relations. Pick the angles that genuinely apply.
5. **Challenges** — real concerns, criticisms or implementation gaps. If there
   genuinely aren't any worth noting, say so briefly rather than inventing
   filler controversy.
6. **Way Forward** — practical, specific steps. Not slogans.
7. **Prelims Pointers** — 3 to 5 one-line facts from this story that are the
   kind of thing an MCQ would actually test.

## Voice

Neutral, factual, analytical — this is a briefing, not an opinion piece.
Sentences under 25 words wherever that's natural. Total length 500-700 words
across all sections; a story that genuinely needs more room can run longer,
but check first whether you're padding rather than adding substance.
